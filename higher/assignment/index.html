🧰 About the assignment

50 marks
out of 160 for the whole course — about 31% of your grade
8 hours
in class, over consecutive lessons, once all of the course has been taught
Closed book
only the Higher data booklet and any data sheets supplied with the task
Set and marked
by Qualifications Scotland, not by your teacher

The assignment gives you an engineering problem in a real context, for example an off-shore wind turbine, a motor racing team or a recording studio. It is split into a number of tasks. Each task tells you the situation, what you have to do and what evidence to hand in. The tasks come from across the course: analogue and digital electronics, programmable control, pneumatics, structures and drive systems can all appear.

In the tasks you will:

  • analyse a problem, for example by drawing a control diagram from a specification;
  • design a solution, for example a logic circuit, an op-amp circuit or a flowchart;
  • build it, by simulating or constructing it;
  • test it, by planning tests, recording results and making changes;
  • evaluate it against the specification.

The rules

  • You work on your own, in class, under a high degree of supervision. You can’t use notes, the internet, email, a phone or AI.
  • You can use normal classroom equipment: simulation software, pneumatics, mechanisms and electronics kits, drawing instruments and a PC.
  • Your teacher can’t help you with the tasks or comment on your work. They can only help with printing and labelling your evidence and making sure you have the right equipment.
  • A new assignment is set every year, and it is only valid for that session.
  • Your work is scanned and marked by Qualifications Scotland markers from the images, so every screenshot and photograph must be clear.

Source: Qualifications Scotland Higher Engineering Science assignment documents, 2024–2026.

🎯 How to get the marks

The Qualifications Scotland course reports for 2024, 2025 and 2026 all say the same thing: candidates are very good at building circuits and systems, but evaluating and testing are the hardest skills. That is where most marks are lost.

Read every task carefully
  • Tasks can look very like ones in earlier assignments but ask for something quite different. Read what each task asks for, and what evidence it wants, before you start.
  • Use the correct engineering terms throughout. The reports ask for “the language and detail expected at Higher level”.
Designing
  • If you are asked for the fewest gates, convert to NAND equivalents and simplify. Many candidates lost marks in 2025 for not doing this. Digital electronics notes
  • When comparing two logic designs for cost or manufacture, count the ICs using the data sheets you are given, not the number of gates. A 7400 IC contains four NAND gates, so four NAND gates cost one IC. Then say what fewer ICs means for cost and manufacture (2026).
Building (simulating or constructing) Usually done well
  • Most candidates get full marks here. Don’t throw marks away on the evidence.
  • Label any substituted components. If you use a different symbol or part because of your software, label it, and make the orientation of components such as diodes, transistors and batteries clear (2025).
  • Screenshots must be clear: components and connections easy to see, values readable and no text cut off. Photographs of a pneumatic build must show the piping clearly enough to be followed (2025, 2026).
  • Practise with your school’s simulation software before the assignment, so you know how to make it do what a task asks.
Testing Most demanding
  • In a test plan, the test is what you do (for example “set Vin to 2.0 V”) and the expected result is what should happen (for example “Vout = −3.0 V”). In 2024 many candidates mixed these up.
  • Record the actual result of every test you carry out.
  • If a test fails, make the amendment before you carry out the next test, and record what you changed. In 2026 some candidates moved on without making the amendment the task asked for.
Evaluating Most demanding
  • Go through the specification point by point. For each one, say whether it is met and give the evidence from your own testing.
  • Refer to how your solution performed, the test results and the amendments you made (2025).
  • Don’t claim that every point is met without evidence. Use the actual performance of your amended solution to justify each decision (2026).
  • When a task asks whether a part can do its job, compare the numbers. For example, state the reaction force at each support and the load it can withstand, then say whether it is safe (2026).

Source: Qualifications Scotland Higher Engineering Science course reports, 2024, 2025 and 2026.

✍️ Writing an evaluation

For every point in the specification: what was required → what your solution actually did (with a value or test result) → met or not met → what you changed or would change.

This made-up example shows the level of detail expected. It is not from a real assignment.

Specification pointWeak evaluationHigher-level evaluation
The fan must switch on above 30 °C.“The fan works so this is met.”“In test 2 the fan switched on at 34 °C, so this point was not met at first. I changed R2 from 2.2 kΩ to 2.7 kΩ, and in the re-test the fan switched on at 30 °C, so this point is now met.”
The output must not exceed 10 V.“Met.”“The highest output in testing was 9.4 V, when the input was 5.0 V. This is below 10 V, so this point is met.”

The weak answers state a decision but give no evidence. The better answers quote the test results and the amendment, so the marker can see why each point is met.
