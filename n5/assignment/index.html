🧰 About the assignment

50 marks
out of 160 for the whole course — about 31% of your grade
8 hours
in class, over consecutive lessons, once all of the course has been taught
Closed book
only the National 4/5 data booklet and any data sheets supplied with the task
Set and marked
by Qualifications Scotland, not by your teacher

The assignment gives you an engineering problem in a real context, split into a number of tasks. Each task tells you the situation, what you have to do and what evidence to hand in. Recent tasks have included system and sub-system diagrams, a microcontroller and flowchart, a sensing circuit, a gear train, a logic circuit and a pneumatic circuit.

In the tasks you will analyse the problem, design a solution, build it by simulating or constructing it, test it and make changes, and evaluate it against the specification.

The rules

  • You work on your own, in class, under a high degree of supervision. You stay in your teacher’s sight, you don’t talk to other candidates, and any posters or displays that might help are covered up.
  • It is closed book: no notes, textbooks, exemplars, internet, email or phone. You can use the National 4/5 data booklet, the data sheets in the task, and normal classroom equipment: simulation software, pneumatics, mechanisms and electronics kits, drawing instruments and a PC.
  • Once you start, you carry on in each lesson until the 8 hours are used up. Your teacher gives out your work at the start of each lesson and collects it at the end. Setting up, clearing away and printing don’t count towards the 8 hours.
  • Your teacher can’t help you with the tasks, give feedback or tell you whether your work is good enough or finished. They can only help you understand the instructions, make sure you have the right equipment, and print and label your evidence. Once you hand the assignment in, it can’t be given back to you for more work.
  • A new assignment is set every year, and it is only valid for that session.

It must be your own work

  • You sign a declaration that the work you hand in is your own, unaided work.
  • Using AI is not allowed. Handing in anything made by a generative AI tool (text, images, code or a circuit) as your own work counts as plagiarism.
  • Copying from another candidate, working with someone else on your tasks, or having a phone, another device or handwritten notes with you during the assignment counts as malpractice, which is taken very seriously.
  • Family and friends can encourage you to prepare, but they must not tell you what to put in your assignment.
  • If you have additional support needs, ask your teacher early about assessment arrangements.

Handing in your evidence

Your work is scanned and marked from images by Qualifications Scotland markers, so it must be easy to read.

  • Write and draw in blue or black permanent ink only.
  • Put the task number at the top and your Scottish Candidate Number (SCN) at the bottom of every page.
  • Don’t glue or fix anything on to the pages, such as photographs. Print them instead.
  • Make every screenshot and photograph clear: components, connections and values must be readable.
  • Every task needs a printed or handwritten copy on the worksheets you are given, even when the task asks you to simulate, construct or code something.
  • Each task tells you how many pages of evidence to expect and roughly how long to spend on it. Use this to plan your 8 hours.
  • Your teacher puts the pages in task order behind a flyleaf, on A4, with one staple in the top-left corner.

Source: Qualifications Scotland National 5 Engineering Science specimen assignment (2024), assignment documents 2024–2026 and Guidance on conditions of assessment for coursework (2023).

🎯 How to get the marks

The course reports for 2024, 2025 and 2026 say that candidates do well at building circuits and completing truth tables, but lose marks on diagrams, test descriptions and evaluations.

Read every task carefully
  • Tasks can look very like ones in earlier assignments but ask for something quite different. Read exactly what each task asks for.
  • If a task asks you to name components, give every name at the same level of detail.
System and sub-system diagrams Most demanding
  • In a system diagram, show the external inputs and outputs of the whole system (for example temperature in, movement out), not a list of components.
  • A sub-system diagram is different. It shows the sub-systems inside the system and how they connect. Don’t just copy the inputs and outputs from the system diagram.
  • Decide whether the system is open loop or closed loop. Only add feedback if there really is a sensor measuring the output. Show every feedback connection, with arrows in the right direction.
  • Practise drawing these diagrams. Every recent report asks for more practice. Systems approach notes
Building (simulating or constructing) Usually done well
  • Check the orientation of every component, especially the battery symbol. This was a common error in 2024 and 2025.
  • For a microcontroller task, choose a suitable microcontroller in your software. Don’t rename its pins to match the task. Your code must match the flowchart and the pin numbers given in the task.
  • For a logic circuit, include suitable input devices and label the inputs clearly so it can be tested.
  • For a pneumatic circuit, make sure your evidence shows that the compressed air supply is connected.
  • For a gear train, label the gear sizes. If a task asks for a compound gear train that reduces speed, check that yours does.
Testing Most demanding
  • Describe expected and actual results in terms of the components named in the task, for example the motor, the LED, the relay or Vout. “It worked” gets no marks.
  • If the task asks for expected results in terms of Vout, give them in terms of Vout.
  • Your test results must match what your simulation actually does.
  • Planned changes must be specific: say exactly what you will change in the flowchart or circuit, and why.
Evaluating and suggesting improvements Most demanding
  • For each point in the specification, say whether it is met and explain why, giving a cause and its effect, for example “the piston outstroked slowly because the flow restrictor limits the exhaust air”.
  • Refer to the specification and the context in the task.
  • An improvement must solve the problem you identified and benefit the person using the system, for example by making it safer.

Source: Qualifications Scotland National 5 Engineering Science course reports, 2024, 2025 and 2026.

✍️ Writing an evaluation

For every point in the specification: was it met? → how do you know (what happened in your test)? → why did it happen (cause and effect)?

This made-up example shows the difference. It is not from a real assignment.

Specification pointWeak evaluationBetter evaluation
The lamp must come on when it gets dark.“Yes, this is met.”“This is met. When I covered the LDR, its resistance increased, so Vout rose to 1.2 V. This switched the transistor on and the lamp came on.”
The gate must open slowly.“The piston goes out.”“This is met. The flow control valve restricts the air leaving the cylinder, so the piston outstrokes slowly and the gate opens slowly.”
