Top Tips

How to set out numerical answers so you don’t lose marks — significant figures, units and prefixes. These apply at National 5, Higher and Advanced Higher.

The advice below is based on the general marking principles that Qualifications Scotland (formerly SQA) publishes with every Engineering Science marking scheme. The principles are the same at all three levels.

🔢 Significant figures

Rounding your final answer is expected — but there is a generous tolerance.

Round your final answer to a sensible number of significant figures. In recent exams the marking schemes almost always give the expected answer to 2 significant figures, and accept anything from one figure fewer to two figures more.

So if the expected answer is given to 2 significant figures, answers with 1, 2, 3 or 4 significant figures get the mark (if the value is right). Copying down every digit on your calculator can lose the mark, because it has far more than two extra figures.

Counting significant figures

  • Start counting at the first non-zero digit. Zeros in front don’t count: 0.018 A has 2 s.f.
  • Zeros between other digits count: 4.07 V has 3 s.f.
  • Zeros at the end of a whole number are unclear: is 210 000 J two figures or six? Writing 210 kJ or 2.1 × 105 J makes it clear — another good reason to use prefixes.
  • To round, look at the next digit: 5 or more, round up; 4 or less, leave it.

How many should I use?

2 significant figures is a safe default — it matches the recent marking schemes and sits in the middle of the accepted window. If a question asks for a specific number of figures or decimal places, do exactly what it asks.

⏱️ When to round

Round the final answer only. Keep the full calculator value for every step in between.

The marking schemes carry the full calculator value through each stage (for example 23.78397106), and only round at the end. If you round too early, your final answer can drift outside the accepted range.

  • Use your calculator’s Ans key or memory to carry values forward rather than retyping a rounded number.
  • If you do write an intermediate value down, write it to at least 4 significant figures.
  • If you make an error (or round) early, you can still earn marks for later steps that are done correctly using your value — markers apply “follow-through”. But it is safer not to rely on it.

📏 Units

A final answer needs its unit. Marks are usually given for “final answer with unit” — a correct number with no unit, or the wrong unit, may not get the mark.
  • Any clear format is accepted: m s−1, m/s or metres per second.
  • Some quantities are ratios and have no unit: efficiency, velocity ratio, gear ratio, current gain hFE, voltage gain, strain and factor of safety.
  • Rotational speed: write rev min−1 or rev s−1. Marking schemes have specifically said do not accept RPM.
  • Stress is often in N mm−2 and Young’s modulus in kN mm−2 — make sure the units of stress and E match before dividing.

Capital or small letter matters. M (mega, a million) and m (milli, a thousandth) differ by a factor of a billion. A 2.2 MΩ resistor is not a 2.2 mΩ resistor!

Converting

  • Big unit → small unit: multiply. 4.7 kΩ = 4.7 × 1000 = 4700 Ω.
  • Small unit → big unit: divide. 18 mA = 18 ÷ 1000 = 0.018 A.
  • On a calculator, type 4.7 kΩ as 4.7×103 (the ×10x or EXP key). The ENG key shows answers in steps of 103, which match the prefixes.
  • Squared units square the prefix: 1 m = 1000 mm, but 1 m2 = 1 000 000 mm2. 1 N mm−2 = 1 MPa.

Shortcuts that keep the prefixes

Some prefix pairs work together, so you can skip the conversion: V ÷ kΩ = mA, and mA × kΩ = V. For example, 12 V ÷ 4.7 kΩ = 2.55 mA. If in doubt, convert everything to base units first.

✍️ Showing your working

Unless a question asks you to show working, a correct final answer with its unit gets full marks on its own. But if the final answer is wrong, working is the only way to pick up marks — marking schemes give marks for the steps along the way.

  1. Write the formula.
  2. Substitute the numbers (in consistent units).
  3. Rearrange (transpose) if needed.
  4. Give the final answer, rounded, with its unit.

If a separate box is given for the final answer, only that box is marked — make sure your answer is in it.

✅ Exam checklist

  • Kept the full calculator value until the end?
  • Rounded the final answer — 2 significant figures is a safe default?
  • Unit included, with the right prefix (and capital/small letter)?
  • Ratio answers (efficiency, gain, factor of safety) left without a unit?
  • Does the answer make sense? (A base current of 3 A, or an efficiency of 140%, means something has gone wrong.)
